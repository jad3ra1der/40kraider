# Decisions
## What were the most important decisions you made during the project, and why did you make them?
The most important decision I made would be the designs of each unit's page. I wanted to show off each unit comprehensively in a single page, and I was able to do that with use of the slideshow container I made in javascript. Being able to display the miniature that I built, as well as the painted miniature on the front of the box and an artistic rendition of each unit I felt was sufficient enough to show the model from different angles. I also included links to the wiki and their stat sheets in-game to provide an even further comprehensive look at them.

# What Worked
## What went well, what are you most satisfied with?
Pretty much everything worked. The website came together very quickly and easily, I was able to nail down a consistent formula for making each page, and everything I tried for the first time ended up working flawlessly. I'm most proud of the slideshow container, as it very easily handled something that I wanted to do but wasn't sure if it was within my scope.

# What you would do differently
## If you started over, what would you change about your approach, tools, or scope?
Not a thing. Everything worked out exactly as I wanted it to, and it isn't broken, so I'm not fixing it.

# What you learned
## What did you learn about building for the web, about yourself, about your tools, or scope?
I reinforced a lot of my knowledge about HTML, as well as gained some experience with Javascript containers and how they interact with webpages. Nothing has changed about myself, I still hate trying to hit word counts. I learned a lot about VS Code's autocorrect, with it being very helpful in making the many pages in collaboration with my consistent file naming scheme, however I found it a bit intrusive as sometimes it would attempt to write code for me instead of providing suggestions. The process was fairly simple and I didn't learn much that I didn't already know.
